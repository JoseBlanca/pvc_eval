# Performance review of the VCF parser, 19 October 2026

This report is about `parse_vcf`, the function of `src/pvc_eval/vcf.py` that reads a VCF
file, checks and trims each record, gives it its span (`doc/spec.md` §5.2) and yields the
records in order of span start. It says where its time and memory go, which changes are
worth trying, and in which order. It is the first review of the parser, made at commit
156dc66 on branch `perf/vcf-parser`, on an Apple M5 Pro with 18 cores and 64 GB, with
CPython 3.15.0 free-threaded (3.15t, GIL off). The experiments it plans are closed here,
one by one, as they are run.

## The workload and the baseline

No real VCF on this machine matches a reference here, so the input is generated by
`benchmarks/make_vcf.py` from the real sequence of GRCh38 chromosome 20: REFs taken from
the genome, a SNP about every 300 bases, indels of 1 to 20 bases (half in repeat runs, some
written with their shared base on the right), 5% of records with several ALTs, and 200
samples whose genotype column holds GT, GQ and PS. `benchmarks/README.md` gives the
commands.

| input | records | span limit | wall time | peak memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chr20:1-5,000,000 | 18,310 | 10 kb (default) | 3.78 ± 0.02 s | 36 MB |
| chr20:1-5,000,000 | 18,310 | none | 4.31 ± 0.04 s | 615 MB |
| chr20, 64 Mb | 234,998 | 10 kb | 47.7 ± 0.3 s | 37 MB |
| chr20, 64 Mb | 234,998 | none | 59.4 ± 3.2 s | 7,612 MB |

Wall times are the mean and standard deviation of 5 hyperfine runs, peak memory the median
of 3 maximum resident set sizes. The machine was not quiet: two stuck test runs each held a
core during these measurements (stopped since), and the small input measured again at the
end ran 2.7% faster. These numbers locate the costs; each experiment measures its own
baseline again beside its candidate.

At the default limit, 27 MB of the 36 MB is the interpreter and its imports; what the parser
holds is 3.6 MiB (tracemalloc). With no limit, the parser holds every record of a contig
until the next contig, as the spec says since the owner's decision of 19 October 2026, and
the memory grows with the contig: 7.6 GB for chromosome 20.

## Where the time and the memory go

**Time.** Parsing the 200 sample columns of each record takes about 81% of the wall time
(Tachyon, wall mode, 15,316 samples; cProfile puts it at 14.3 of 15.0 profiled seconds).
`_parse_genotype` is called 3,662,000 times on the small input, once per sample. Reading
lines, gzip, the FASTA window and the span search together take under 0.2 s. The wall and
cpu profiles agree on functions, so the parser does not wait; they disagree on single lines
inside the genotype loop, so the shares of single lines are not trusted until the profile
is taken again (measurement 2, below).

**Memory with no limit.** A held record costs about 31 KB: per sample, a `VcfGenotype` of
64 bytes and its own tuple of alleles of 64 bytes, and the phase set's string. Nothing is
shared between samples: the small input holds 3,662,000 distinct tuples of alleles for 10
distinct values. The extra time with no limit (0.5 s on the small input, 7 to 11 s on
chromosome 20) is mostly the garbage collector walking those objects: 25 collections taking
0.47 s, against 9 taking 0.02 s when the objects are shared (measured by the memory
reviewer).

## The measurement plan

What has to be in place before an experiment is judged, in the order it unblocks them:

1. **An output to compare.** The timed script prints only the number of records, so a change
   that altered spans or genotypes would print the same. A second script, not timed, prints
   the count and a sha256 of every record in the order emitted. Every experiment compares it
   with its baseline's.
2. **Profiles taken again on the 64 Mb input**, wall and cpu mode, 30 seconds each, twice per
   mode, by the owner (Tachyon needs root). A 47-second run makes the first second lost to
   the attach negligible and gives steadier shares per line.
3. **Two more workloads.** The generated genotype column is lighter than real callers'
   (GT:GQ:PS, about 10 bytes per sample, where GATK and DeepVariant write AD, DP and PL, 25 to
   40 bytes), and every sample of a record shares one phase set, which flatters any sharing
   of genotypes; real callers give each sample its own. So: a variant of the 5 Mb input with
   a GATK-like column and a phase set per sample; and a one-sample input over chromosome 20,
   about one record per 650 bases, the common case of comparing one sample against a truth
   set, where the per-record work, not the per-sample one, will dominate.
4. **The noise on each input an experiment claims on**, measured twice apart, and each
   candidate timed against its baseline in one hyperfine call that alternates them.

## The candidates, in the order they will be tried

P1 and P2 come from three reviewers who found the same thing apart (algorithms, interpreter,
memory).

**P1. Parse each distinct GT once** (`vcf.py:310-344`). What a GT parses to, its alleles and
whether it is phased, or a sentinel, depends only on the GT's text and the record's number
of alleles; the small input has 24 such pairs for 3,662,000 samples. A bounded cache on a
small pure function `_parse_gt(gt, num_alleles)` parses each once, and every genotype with
the same GT shares one immutable tuple of alleles. Trial by the algorithms reviewer: 3,661,976
of 3,662,000 lookups hit the cache, the records are identical (same sha256), and with no limit
the held bytes fall from 540 to 317 MiB. It holds on real data too, since its key does not
include the phase set or the quality. Code: about the same, perhaps clearer. Expected to be
accepted on time and memory both.

*What P1 gave* (commit 6aa06a1, measured 19 October 2026 from 15:30 to 15:50). The code
adds 28 lines and removes 8: `_parse_gt`, under `functools.lru_cache(maxsize=1024)`, gives
the alleles and the phasing, or a sentinel, and `_parse_genotype` builds the `VcfGenotype`
from it with the sample's own PS and GQ. One test checks that two samples with the same GT
share one alleles tuple. The four digests equal the README's. On the 5 Mb GT:GQ:PS input,
cProfile counts 24 calls of `_parse_gt`'s body, 44 of `_is_allele_index` instead of
7,104,558, and 42.3 million calls in all instead of 85.5 million; the cache reports
3,661,976 hits and 24 misses. Each input was timed in one hyperfine call that alternated
base and P1, 10 runs (5 for the 64 Mb input), mean ± sd:

| input | span limit | base | P1 | change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 Mb, GT:GQ:PS | 10 kb | 3.501 ± 0.016 s | 2.762 ± 0.021 s | −21.1% |
| 5 Mb, GT:GQ:PS | none | 4.056 ± 0.024 s | 3.175 ± 0.087 s | −21.7% |
| 5 Mb, GATK-like | 10 kb | 3.963 ± 0.029 s | 3.198 ± 0.106 s | −19.3% |
| 5 Mb, GATK-like | none | 4.513 ± 0.026 s | 3.642 ± 0.046 s | −19.3% |
| one sample | 10 kb | 767.1 ± 4.8 ms | 749.2 ± 4.1 ms | −2.3% |
| one sample | none | 851.6 ± 4.9 ms | 831.4 ± 13.2 ms | −2.4% |
| 64 Mb | 10 kb | 44.169 ± 0.394 s | 34.484 ± 0.075 s | −21.9% |

The one-sample input, timed again, gave 759.6 and 747.1 ms (−1.7%): a gain below the noise,
as expected where a record has one sample. Peak memory with no limit, the median of 3
maximum resident set sizes (1 for the 64 Mb input): 615 MB to 383 MB on the 5 Mb GT:GQ:PS
input (−38%), 610 MB to 380 MB on the GATK-like one (−38%), and 7,612 MB to 4,611 MB on
the 64 Mb input (−39%), where the run also took 40.3 s instead of 51.5 s. At the default
limit the peak is unchanged, 34 MB. The gain holds on the GATK-like input, whose samples
have phase sets of their own. The one-minute load average was 1.25 to 1.90 during the small
inputs, 1.59 to 3.18 during the 64 Mb input and the repeat, and 3.08 to 3.55 during the
memory runs, when `mediaanalysisd` took one core.

**P2. Share whole identical genotypes** (`vcf.py:310`). A bounded cache over all of
`_parse_genotype`'s arguments returns one frozen `VcfGenotype` for every equal genotype.
Simulated by the memory reviewer on the small input with no limit: 2.7 KB per held record
instead of 31 KB, 88 MB of peak memory instead of 614 MB, and the records equal. But its hit
rate depends on the phase set and the quality repeating, which they do here only because
the generator shares the phase set; on real phased data the reviewer estimates 11 KB per
record. It is tried after P1, on the workload with a phase set per sample, and kept only if
the gain over P1 holds there.

**P3. Find the FORMAT keys once per record** (`vcf.py:528-537`). Each sample now builds a
small dict of its fields and reads three keys from it, 11 million `dict.get` calls on the
small input; the positions of GT, GQ and PS can be found once per record and each sample
read by position. Same output on this input; with a key written twice in FORMAT, the dict
keeps the last value and an index the first, which the change must decide. The code gets a
little more complicated (dropped trailing fields need handling), so it is kept only for a
gain above the noise, measured after P1.

**P4. Threads over batches of records** (`vcf.py:501`, the owner's decision). Genotypes
depend only on their record's fields, so a pool of threads can parse batches of records while
the main thread keeps the window, the spans and the order. A prototype by the parallelism
reviewer, on the small input: 3.53 s on the present code, 1.05 s with 8 threads, 0.71 s with
16, the output identical record for record and the GIL off; but peak memory 165 MB at 8
threads against 35 MB, or 67 MB with smaller batches at 1.19 s. About 30 lines and a
`num_threads` parameter. It trades memory for time, so it goes to the owner with both
numbers, measured after P1 to P3, since those may change what is left to share among
threads.

**P5. A genotype record that is not frozen** (`vcf.py:62-76`, the owner's decision). The
frozen dataclass's constructor sets each field through `object.__setattr__`, four calls per
genotype, about 10% of the cpu profile. Not freezing it removes them and the guarantee that a
genotype cannot change after it is made, which the coding rules ask for. Not to be tried
unless the owner wants that trade; P2 would remove most of these constructions anyway.

**Not proposed.** Splitting a record's samples among threads: measured slower than batching
records (0.58 s against 0.30 s at 8 threads). Sharing equal phase-set strings within a record:
the gain comes from the generator giving all samples one phase set. Compiled code (numpy, C)
for the sample columns: outside pure Python, so the owner's decision; nobody has measured it.

## What the code already does well

The window and the span search cost little: the span is searched in a stretch that doubles
from 64 bases, the window keeps one buffer, and both together take about 0.1 s of 3.8 on the
small input. At the default limit the parser's memory does not grow with the input: 36.0 MB
for 5 Mb, 36.6 MB for 64 Mb.

## Baseline, measured again

The baseline above was taken while two stuck test runs each held a core. It was measured
again on 19 October 2026 between 15:02 and 15:26, at commit df41b16, whose code of
pvc_eval is that of ac58b60, on the four inputs `benchmarks/README.md` lists. Two of the
inputs are new: the 5 Mb region with a GATK-like genotype column (GT:AD:DP:GQ:PL:PS, a
phase set per sample, an INFO of about 250 bytes), and all of chromosome 20 with one
sample and a record about every 650 bases. Wall times are the mean ± sd of 5 hyperfine runs
(3 for the 64 Mb input with no limit), and peak memory the median of 3 maximum resident set
sizes (1 for that input).

| input | records | span limit | wall time | peak memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chr20:1-5,000,000, GT:GQ:PS | 18,310 | 10 kb | 3.540 ± 0.141 s | 36.0 MB |
| chr20:1-5,000,000, GT:GQ:PS | 18,310 | none | 4.013 ± 0.013 s | 614.9 MB |
| chr20:1-5,000,000, GATK-like | 18,127 | 10 kb | 3.947 ± 0.017 s | 35.8 MB |
| chr20:1-5,000,000, GATK-like | 18,127 | none | 4.517 ± 0.024 s | 610.1 MB |
| chr20, one sample, GATK-like | 98,149 | 10 kb | 767.1 ± 2.4 ms | 34.0 MB |
| chr20, one sample, GATK-like | 98,149 | none | 861.5 ± 15.2 ms | 128.8 MB |
| chr20, 64 Mb, GT:GQ:PS | 234,998 | 10 kb | 44.280 ± 0.479 s | 36.7 MB |
| chr20, 64 Mb, GT:GQ:PS | 234,998 | none | 53.102 ± 1.283 s | 7,611.9 MB |

The noise, each input at the default limit timed again 10 to 12 minutes after the first:

| input | first | second | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 Mb, GT:GQ:PS | 3.540 ± 0.141 s | 3.372 ± 0.023 s | −4.7% |
| 5 Mb, GATK-like | 3.947 ± 0.017 s | 3.833 ± 0.034 s | −2.9% |
| one sample | 767.1 ± 2.4 ms | 734.0 ± 3.9 ms | −4.3% |
| 64 Mb | 44.280 ± 0.479 s | 44.608 ± 0.312 s | +0.7% |

The three small inputs all ran 3% to 5% faster the second time. The first run of the
5 Mb GT:GQ:PS input includes one run of 3.79 s among four between 3.45 and 3.50 s. So on
the small inputs a candidate claims a gain only above about 5%, and only when it is timed
against its baseline in one hyperfine call that alternates them, as item 4 of the
measurement plan says. On the 64 Mb input the two measurements differ by 0.7%.

The machine was quiet this time. The one-minute load average was 1.69 before the first
run, between 0.75 and 2.42 during the measurements, and 0.78 after the last. Before and
after each run, no process used more than 50% of a core. Against the first baseline, the
5 Mb input at the default limit is 6.3% faster (3.540 s against 3.777 s) and the 64 Mb
input 7.1% faster (44.280 s against 47.666 s). The peak memory is unchanged.
